# Decisions We've Made (Standing Rules)

*The short list of things we've all agreed on and why, so we don't re-litigate them every
week. The full detailed history (every decision, with dates) lives in the repo's decision log
for Tenzing's reference — this page is the plain-English summary that actually matters day to day.*

## 1. Options are paper-only until a strategy proves itself in the expensive-options world
We can model and visualize options for learning, but no real options money until a strategy
survives testing in the harsh options cost environment. Our own testing shows naive options
strategies lose almost everything to fees and time decay. Being right about direction is not
enough — see the Glossary's "theta decay."

## 2. No outside money, ever, in the current structure
This is Tenzing's own money, run with Claude subagents. We're not building something where
strangers give us money to manage — that would make us an unregistered investment fund, which
is a legal problem we don't need. If we ever build a club, it's an education/research club
(the "Fordham club" idea), not a fund.

## 3. Money stays separate from software
Capital stays in index funds or fake-money (paper) trading until a strategy clears the one
rule (beats buy-and-hold and random, out of sample, after costs). We build and test first; we
never fund something just because it sounds promising.

## 4. Honesty is the asset
The results log never gets edited to make us look better. "We tested 10 things and 9 failed"
is a more valuable, more credible record than a fake winner — both for making good decisions
and for what it says about us in an interview.

## 5. The stock test list stays small, clean, and boring on purpose
As of 2026-07-06 we deliberately cut the test list from ~97 stocks down to 30 well-known,
liquid, easy-to-explain companies (no penny stocks, no leveraged/risky ETFs). A bigger list
isn't automatically better — it just means more chances for a fake pattern to show up by
chance, and it's harder to reason about. Quality over quantity. See Results So Far for the list.

## 6. New trading ideas try the "cheap" version first
Before investing real effort (or money) into a fancier version of an idea, we test the
simplest version first and only go further if it actually shows promise. Don't pay for
complexity before the simple version earns it.
