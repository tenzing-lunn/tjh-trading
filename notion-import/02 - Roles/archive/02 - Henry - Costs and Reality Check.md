# Henry — Costs, Execution & Measurement ("The Reality Check")

*You do not need to code. You make sure our results reflect the real market, not a clean
fantasy — and you keep the honest scorecard of whether we're actually beating the index.*

## Your one-line mission
Own the brutal realities that kill most strategies: trading costs, what's actually tradable,
and honest measurement against just buying the index. If Jonathan is the offense, you're the
accountant proving whether the offense actually scored *after the bills are paid.*

## What you own
- **Cost assumptions.** Real spreads, slippage, and fees for whatever we'd trade.
- **The stock list.** What we're allowed to test/trade, and why.
- **The track record.** The honest record of what we tested and whether it beat SPY.

## Your actual to-do list, in order
1. **Build the real cost table.** For each thing we'd trade (ETF, single stock, option), find
   realistic spread + slippage + fee numbers, in basis points, from real broker/market data —
   not guesses. **This is the single most important input to whether a result is real.**
2. **Approve the stock list.** Which tickers, why, and what to exclude (penny stocks,
   pre-IPO junk, anything too illiquid to actually trade at the quoted price). Tenzing just
   proposed a cleaner 30-stock list — see **What To Do Right Now** — that's yours to ratify.
3. **Own the track record — the judgment, not the typing.** The engine automatically logs
   *what* was tested and the result. Your column is the part a machine can't fill in: *is
   this real, or luck?* Flag anything suspicious (too few trades, only works in one market
   condition, cherry-picked stocks) before anyone believes a good number.
4. **Keep a trade journal**, once anything is paper-traded: what we expected vs. what actually
   happened, and why.

## How your work is judged
- **Costs are non-negotiable.** If your numbers are too optimistic, every result becomes a
  lie. When in doubt, assume the *worse* cost — survive the worst case, not the best case.
- **Every result is compared to just holding SPY.** "We made 4%" means nothing alone. "We
  made 4% while SPY made 9%, after costs" is the truth.
- **Never edit the log to make us look good.** A clean record of "we tested 10 things, 9
  failed" is more valuable and more credible than a fake winner.

## What you're here to learn
How real trading costs work, how to benchmark performance honestly, and the discipline of
measuring things accurately even when the truth is disappointing. In a finance interview,
"I built the cost model and proved most of our strategies didn't beat the index" is a
*stronger* signal than claiming you found a money printer.

## The trap for you specifically
Being too generous with cost assumptions because pessimistic costs "kill" an exciting-looking
strategy. That's backwards — costs that are too low are exactly how retail traders convince
themselves they have an edge, then lose money for real. Your pessimism protects the group.
