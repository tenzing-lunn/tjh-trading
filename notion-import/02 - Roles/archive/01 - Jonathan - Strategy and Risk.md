# Jonathan — Strategy & Risk ("The Offense")

*You do not need to code. Your job is to think clearly about why a trading edge would exist,
and how much we should bet if it does.*

## Your one-line mission
Supply testable trading ideas with a real economic reason behind them, and the risk rules
that keep one bad trade from hurting the group. Tenzing can build and test any idea — your
job is making sure the idea isn't just noise dressed up as a signal.

## What you own
- **Strategy ideas ("theses").** The hypotheses we test.
- **Position sizing & risk rules.** How much we'd put on a given trade, and when we'd cut a loser.

## Your actual to-do list, in order
1. **Write strategy ideas**, one short page each — see the **Strategy Ideas (Theses)** section
   and copy the template. A real idea is NOT "buy when the price crosses a line." It's: *what
   real-world behavior creates this pattern, who is losing money to it, and why hasn't
   everyone already figured this out?*
2. **Define sizing rules.** For anything we'd actually fund: max % of the pool per position,
   max total exposure at once, and when we'd cut a loser. Plain rules Tenzing can turn into code.
3. **Predict before you see the result.** Before Tenzing runs your idea, write down what you
   *expect* to happen. Being wrong on the record is how you learn what actually drives returns.
4. **Review results with skepticism.** When something looks like it works, your job is to
   argue *why it might be fake* (lucky timing, cherry-picked data) before anyone believes it.

## How your work is judged
- **An idea with no "why it exists" gets bounced back.** "It backtested well" is not a reason
  — that's the trap (curve-fitting).
- **Good reasoning beats a good result.** An idea that's well-argued but fails the test is a
  *success* — you learned something true. A vague idea that happened to pass is worthless.

## What you're here to learn
How to turn a market hunch into a testable claim, how to size a bet against the risk of
ruin, and how to read a real result without fooling yourself. This is the core skill of a
quant researcher — and it's exactly what you can talk about in a finance interview.

## The trap for you specifically
Falling in love with an idea because it's elegant or because it happened to backtest well.
The market doesn't pay for elegance. Assume every idea is fake until it survives testing on
data it's never seen, after real costs — and stay a little suspicious even then.
