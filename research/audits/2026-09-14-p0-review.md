## 3. P0.6 — `scan.py` verdict — CONFIRMED on the gate; two design notes

vs own-B&H (N = 1) AND vs SPY-through-the-same-folds (N = 1) AND after a scan-level discount
charged on `len(rows)` = 93 with the cross-row *active* Sharpes as the trial distribution;
`clean` adds `ret > 0`, ≥ 30 trades/fold, `rand_pct ≥ 0.95`; `suspect` = significant on one

**Full run** (`python3 scan.py`, 31 tickers × 3 = 93 backtests, 83 s):
`0 EDGE? | 3 suspect | 90 dead`. The audit's named beta-passers, with the legs behind the printed
`minDSR = 0.00` (from a 5-ticker re-run; `dsr_scan` there is charged on 15 rows, the two
benchmark legs are unaffected):

| Row | OOS ret | active vs B&H | `dsr_vs_bh` | `dsr_vs_spy` | `dsr_scan` | rand % | t/fold | verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| NVDA / sma | +3165 % | −43 % | 0.280 | 0.999 | 0.010 | 98 | 1.2 | suspect |
| NVDA / tsmom | +2885 % | −49 % | 0.234 | 0.999 | 0.006 | 99 | 9.0 | suspect |
| LLY / tsmom | +619 % | −46 % | 0.059 | 0.939 | 0.000 | 97 | 6.2 | dead |
| AVGO / tsmom | +609 % | −58 % | 0.017 | 0.951 | 0.000 | 98 | 8.0 | suspect |
| META / tsmom (the audit's near-miss) | +337 % | −26 % | 0.506 | 0.836 | 0.042 | 95 | 12.0 | dead |

None passes the own-B&H leg; META/tsmom — which under the old rule needed only more trades to
print `EDGE?` — is now dead on the statistics, not on the trade-count heuristic. That is the
behaviour §2.2 asked for. The closing text now names the N actually applied (93).

**Note 1 — the random baseline is not holding-period matched; the original audit's formula is
off by 2.** §2.5 prescribed `k = len(combined) / num_trades` and the implementation uses it
(L117). But `compute_metrics` counts every bar with non-zero turnover as a trade, and a coin
redrawn every `k` bars at p = 0.5 changes state on only half its redraws, so:

| Candidate | trades | k = n/trades | random(k) mean trades (40 seeds) | random(k/2) mean trades |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NVDA / tsmom | 45 | 37.2 | 22.9 | 46.7 |
| AAPL / meanrev | 229 | 7.3 | 120.5 | 282.6 |
| MSFT / sma | 12 | 139.6 | 5.8 | 12.4 |

The null trades about half as often as the candidate, so it pays about half the costs and its
percentile is a *harder* bar — the error is conservative and cannot manufacture an `EDGE?`. But
the column is labelled "holding-period-matched" and it is not. Fix: `k = len(bh_comb) /
(2 * num_trades)`. This is the reviewer's own spec error, faithfully implemented.

**Note 2 — `rand_pct` measures exposure, not timing (spec gap, not an implementation error).**
With `p_long = 0.5` the null is long half the time. In a name that rose 10–30×, any candidate
that is long more than half the time beats most coin flips on active-vs-B&H with no timing at
all: NVDA 98–99 %, AVGO 98 %, LLY 97 %, JPM 98 %, MSFT 98 %; the mean-reversion rows (low or
short exposure) score 0–18 %. Because `rand_pct` is an AND-condition behind the active DSR
legs it cannot create a false `EDGE?`, but the docstring's claim that "what's compared is timing
skill, not turnover" is not yet true. Recommended refinement (beyond §2.5): draw the null with
`p_long` equal to the candidate's mean |position| over the OOS window, so exposure *and*
turnover are matched. §7 item 6.

**Note 3 — what "suspect" now means.** All three suspects are "significant vs SPY only"
(NVDA/AVGO went up more than the index; their own-B&H legs are 0.02–0.28). That is the audit's
rule, applied correctly, but the printed table shows only `minDSR` and — because of §1's missed
item — the red flags do not say which leg passed. A reader cannot tell why NVDA/sma is
"suspect" rather than "dead". Fixing §7 item 1 fixes this too.

`verdict_log.scan_row_to_record` now logs `dsr_vs_bh / dsr_vs_spy / dsr_scan / rand_pct` and
keeps `deflated_sharpe` = the binding leg. Correct.

## 5. P0.8 — `sanity_check.py` #8 / #9 — CONFIRMED

`python3 sanity_check.py`: **12/12 pass**, 2 m 57 s (the commit's runtime note is accurate).

**#8 construction.** `synthetic_ohlcv(kappa=0.0, drift=…)` is a log-price random walk with
constant drift: with κ = 0 there is no reversion to `level`, so no strategy can have a genuine
timing edge — the only source of return is the drift, i.e. beta. That is exactly the trap.

The parameter deviation, tested at both settings over 40 seeds:

| Params | price ends higher | raw DSR ≥ 0.95 (B&H) | theory: z = drift/σ·√(n−1) → P(pass) | active DSR refuses random |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| audit's `n=1500, drift=0.0005` | 33 / 40 | **23 / 40 (57.5 %)** | z = 1.61 → ≈ 0.49 | 40 / 40 |
| shipped `n=2500, drift=0.001` | 40 / 40 | **40 / 40** | z = 4.17 → ≈ 0.99 | 40 / 40 |

The deviation is warranted: at the audit's literal spec the first assertion is a coin flip and
the check would fail on ~half of seeds. But the commit's stated reason — "only ~57 % of seeds
trend up at all" — mislabels the statistic: 57 % is the *raw-DSR pass rate*; 83 % of those
paths trend up. The in-line comment in `sanity_check.py` L105–107 repeats the mislabel. Worth a
one-line wording fix (§7 item 7), nothing more.

Does it still exercise the same failure mode? Yes, and more sharply than the shipped assertion
shows. On the shipped series the active gate also refuses candidates that carry *no* cost bleed:

| Candidate on the trending series | raw DSR (vs 0) | active DSR vs B&H | frictionless active DSR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| daily coin flip, seed 1 (as shipped) | 0.942 | 0.006 | 0.038 |
| coin flip redrawn every 60 bars | 0.933 | 0.005 | 0.006 |
| **constant half-long (pure beta, zero turnover)** | **0.998** | **0.002** | 0.002 |

So the refusal is about beta, not about the coin flip's turnover costs (§2.4's own objection to
single-seed randoms does not undercut this check). Optional strengthening: add the half-long
variant as the cleaner second assertion.

**#9 construction.** Seeds vary: `seed = outer*1000 + i`, `outer` 0–99, `i` 0–29 — 3000 distinct
seeds; the panels for `outer = 0` and `outer = 1` are verified different. Calibration over 30
noise panels here:

| statistic | value |
|---|---|
| t: mean / sd / min / max | −0.31 / 1.06 / −2.65 / +1.59 |
| seeds with t < 2 | 30 / 30 |
| seeds with t ≤ −2 | 3 / 30 |
| seeds with \|t\| ≥ 2 | 3 / 30 |

`t < 2` (one-sided) is the correct test under the audit's framing: the question is "does the
gate manufacture *momentum*", and a strongly negative t (momentum losing to EW because it pays
turnover on noise) is a correct "no momentum" answer. A two-sided `abs(t) < 2` would have
failed 10 % of these seeds for the wrong reason. The mildly negative mean is the cost drag; sd ≈
1 says the statistic is calibrated.
